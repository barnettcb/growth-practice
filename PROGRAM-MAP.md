## A. Purpose, boundaries, and evidence rules

This document is the program's durable, revisable **selection map**. It gathers relevant *user-reported* interests and obstacles, maps them against selected reputable methods, shows prerequisites and tradeoffs, and keeps provisional choices visible. It is not the chronological session log, not the RO Journal, and not an automatic characterization of the user or spouse.

**Critical correction (October 17):** The user says **occasional yelling/outbursts are extremely rare and not the primary issue**. Everyday noticing, defensiveness, communication, attention to other people's needs, taking initiative, remembering, follow-through, flexibility, meaningful activity, and connection deserve at least as much attention. Prior writeups sometimes over-weighted exceptional conflict events. **Current frequency and impact must be reconfirmed before prioritizing.**

**Evidence labels** used throughout:
- **U — User report or explicit program preference.** Direct report; not independent proof of motives or diagnoses.
- **H — Historical report/record.** May be informative but not proof of the present state. Indicate its date if materially relevant.
- **S — Source-supported method.** Procedure exists in the named book/manual or reputable source; does not establish that it will help this user.
- **I — Working hypothesis.** Tentative possibility to test; never replace U with I.
- **? — Unverified.** Missing or inconsistent information; do not fill by inference.
- **O — Observed rehearsal in a chat.** Distinct from reported and sustained daily-life behavior.

**Frequency, importance, and fit are not scored yet.** Source-model fit in the tables is an *educational match to a stated problem*, not a personalized efficacy prediction. Changes in goals, clinical assignment, or the user's current account supersede historical notes.

## C. Cross-model *selection matrix*: overlapping goals, different tools

| Function that needs developing | Relevant primary-source skills | Differences / choosing rule | Supporting sources and caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Notice / interrupt an action** | Linehan STOP; DBT Observe/Describe | STOP is a brief behavioral interruption, especially under rising impulses. Observe/Describe builds attention and discrimination. | Fruzzetti Ch. 2 describes noticing judgments and self/other experience. ACT attention/defusion does not itself supply a complete communication response. |
| **Choose the next response** | Linehan Wise Mind, Effectively, interpersonal objective/relationship/self-respect priorities | Wise Mind integrates emotional and reasonable information; it is *not* the specific words to say. Choice of response depends on situation, goal, relationship, and boundaries. | Fruzzetti Ch. 3 offers pre-rehearsed non-escalating alternatives. ACT Choice Point/values can orient the action without requiring the thought or feeling to disappear. |
| **Respond without arguing an interpretation as fact** | DBT Describe/nonjudgmentally; ACT cognitive defusion; CBT examining evidence | DBT description separates observation from appraisal; ACT changes *relationship to a thought*; CBT tests/revises the thought when useful. None automatically proves an accusation true/false. | *Mind Over Mood* Chs. 6–11; *Learning ACT* Chs. 3–4; *ACT with Love* Chs. 5, 8–10. |
| **Handle missed intentions** | Linehan Missing-Links; ACT committed action; CBT action planning | First ask *what link was missing*: knowledge, willingness, remembering, implementation. Select matching environment/behavioral solution; don't default to shame, avoidance, or willpower. | *Learning ACT* Ch. 7; *Mind Over Mood* Chs. 5, 10–11; trial of reminders/implementation cues only when a memory link is actually implicated. |
| **Listen / communicate / validate** | Fruzzetti Chs. 2, 4, 6–8; DBT GIVE/FAST/DEAR MAN; Harris Chs. 14–17 | Fruzzetti centers emotional arousal, attunement and validation; DBT distinguishes objective/relationship/self-respect effectiveness; ACT couples work connects communication with chosen values. | *Reconcilable Differences* Chs. 7–14 adds acceptance of differences and communication patterns, with couple-therapy evidence. Not all couple exercises transfer to solo practice. |
| **Initiate care and build connection** | ACT values/committed action; Harris appreciation, giving, play chapters; behavioral activation/action plans | Actions can be selected by values and performed ahead of complaints. Use actual preferences/context; do not turn caring gestures into a rigid approval-contingent checklist. | Fruzzetti mindful attention; optional self-compassion exercises. Results are not solely the user's responsibility. |
| **Meet emotion without overexplaining / self-attack** | ACT willingness and defusion; DBT emotion regulation; CBT emotion naming/cognitive tools; mindful self-compassion | Different mechanisms; select based on observed barrier. Do not treat painful emotion as evidence an interpretation is true; do not try every exercise at once. | *Mindful Self-Compassion Workbook* particularly Chs. 6–8, 11, 16–20; historical guilt/shame reports need current verification. |
| **Openness and rigidity when warranted** | RO-DBT flexibility and social-signaling procedures | May be useful when *context and observed behavior* support it, but not as universal explanation. | Lynch *Theory and Practice* for rationale; official RO handouts for steps. Therapist-directed work takes priority when relevant. |

### Provisional sequencing (capability-based; **not** a fixed eight-week syllabus)

**Learning Unit 1 — Noticing, stopping, and choosing an effective ordinary response.** Essential: STOP; Wise Mind; Observe/Describe/nonjudgment; Fruzzetti Ch. 2 foundation and Ch. 3 alternative-response rehearsal; Missing-Links for intended practice and Chain Analysis only when the question concerns an enacted ineffective response. ACT Choice Point/defusion as deliberately brief comparison or next-unit candidate, not silently required in full. **Context balance:** include work, neutral feedback, genuine mistakes, and everyday relationship issues; do not build most practice around screaming or extreme distress.

**Potential next unit — Remembering, initiating, and following through.** Strong candidate based on broad reported needs, but Monday review should select or defer. Prerequisites: define a concrete intended action, know how to distinguish nonoccurrence from avoidance, match cues and commitments to actual barriers. Possible Linehan Missing-Links plus ACT committed action/CBT behavioral design and relational initiative.

**Other candidate units:** (a) attentional presence/listening/understanding and non-scripted validation; (b) flexible disagreement and defensiveness after listening; (c) emotional literacy and willingness; (d) positive connection and self-directed enjoyment; (e) repair/boundaries; (f) consolidation. Their order remains open, and many may be paired if properly justified. **Calendar Mondays continue, even if the current learning unit remains active for several weeks.**

### Prerequisite quality gate (for every unit and every source)

1. Identify **named skill**, actual primary source/page, prerequisites the source explicitly calls for, and its intended purpose.
2. Mark **essential** (teach first), **useful supplement** (could be concurrent), **advanced/defer** (only after enough basics), **not applicable** (with reason), and **unverified**.
3. Compare alternatives *across* models for the **same functional need**, not only shared words (e.g., 'mindfulness' or 'acceptance'). Include genuine differences, limits, evidence uncertainty, accessibility and likely rehearsal burden.
4. Choose a **minimum coherent instruction + rehearsal package**. Thoroughly *review* the relevant options; don't automatically *assign* every option.
5. Trace **each important taught capability** to a rehearsal, real-life application, or a correctly attributed analytic worksheet. Track reading and actual practice separately.
6. Revisit if rehearsal/real-life evidence contradicts the tentative fit. Do not claim person–therapy matching is scientifically established from informal observations.

## E. How future curricula should be decided and updated

**After each Monday review** (even if a learning unit continues): retrieve relevant current entries and source decisions; discuss actual experiences including positive or uneventful days; identify current target and distinction between *new problem*, *unchanged problem*, and *insufficient observations*; decide continue, deepen, revisit prerequisites, add practice, or start a different unit. Store a **Next Learning Unit Brief** in the chronological log. **Do not automatically increment unit number.**

**Before each deep dive:** review this map selectively, conduct a focused new source/prerequisite/alternative audit appropriate to the chosen unit; distinguish verified reading from suggested further sources. Provide a source table and practice coverage table. Update this map only when an enduring issue/priority/method assessment actually changes—not after every ephemeral chat.

**Every ~4 Monday reviews, and sooner after meaningful new evidence:** revisit priority order, models considered, reported changes and counterexamples, abandoned/unfinished skills, broad life domains, and fit hypotheses. Refer to dated log records. When changes to a previous conclusion are needed, preserve a short dated change note rather than rewriting the historical report as fact.

## G. Spiral curriculum: retention and combination map (2026-10-17)

**Confirmed design decision:** Skills remain eligible for later retrieval and practice after their original unit is no longer the active topic. This is a **teaching design**, not new information about the user's capabilities. The source methods retain their own attribution. Space/Library continuity records—not a calendar-week number or unchecked app worksheet—are the evidence of actual study or rehearsal.

| Introduced skill or skill cluster | What can be revisited later (when actually taught) | Potential meaningful later context | Evidence that must not be assumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linehan Observe / Describe; Wise Mind | Brief unaided explanation; separate event from interpretation; identify factors for a wise next choice | Ordinary work feedback; noticing another person's needs; later communication training | Spontaneous recognition or performance under real pressure |
| Linehan STOP | Rehearse a purposeful pause, including mid-explanation, then choose a next act | Later ACT flexibility, listening, respectful disagreement and follow-through | That repeating an acronym means it was used in real life |
| Fruzzetti Chapter 2 / Chapter 3 practices | Brief description rather than judgment; anticipated cue → rehearsed alternative; urge without automatic action | Later attentive listening, validation, and repair; ordinary work/home interactions | That the other person reciprocates or an imagined response works in a real event |
| Missing-Links (distinct from Chain Analysis) | Analyze why a planned, effective behavior was not performed; choose a barrier-matched adjustment | Later initiative, homework, commitments and rehearsal consistency | That any missed action was motivated by avoidance or unwillingness |
| Later ACT, CBT, RO-DBT, and relationship skills | Only after source-grounded teaching, selectively review the actual named method with context-fit alternatives | New learning-unit scenarios across work, relationships, values and positive activities | That thematic overlap makes two therapies the same procedure |

**Review selection:** At the Monday review, identify the current skill and at most one or two previously introduced skills to revisit next. Choose by learning relevance, recency, uncertainty and actual gaps, not an automated skill score. In a short rehearsal, one current focus plus one earlier skill is generally enough, and single-skill practice remains valid. Prefer periodic unaided retrieval with corrective feedback over repeatedly rereading the full chapter. Vary scenarios once steps are understood; no forced escalation into rare extreme situations.

**Spaced-practice evidence:** Dunlosky et al. (2013) rate practice testing and distributed practice highly for general learning; interleaving has more qualified/variable evidence. Pashler et al. (2007) recommend spacing and integrating explanation with practice. These findings inform the design but **do not prove** an optimal clinical schedule or that any exercise produces durable behavioral change for this user. See https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266 and https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1 .

**Initial retention roster:** The October 16 guided STOP rehearsal exists in the continuity log. Do not infer study, successful recall or rehearsal of Wise Mind, Fruzzetti Chapter 2/3 or Missing-Links merely because the reconstructed Unit 1 syllabus now includes them. The first cumulative selection should follow actual Unit 1 study and relevant review records. No automated due dates or mastery levels are assigned.
