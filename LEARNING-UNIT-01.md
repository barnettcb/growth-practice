# Learning Unit 01 — Notice, Pause, and Choose a Workable Response

**Version:** 2.4 (source-specific incident review and resume-first starter), October 17, 2026.  
**Status:** Assigned *learning material*, **not completed**. This rebuild supersedes the former calendar 'Week 1' teaching outline.  
**Pacing:** May span multiple calendar weeks and Tuesday training chats. Monday review occurs regardless of completion.  
**Focus:** Everyday automatic explaining/correcting and difficulty choosing what to do next; also applies to work, neutral feedback, minor disappointment and ordinary misunderstandings. Occasional severe escalation is a *non-default* optional context, not the center of the unit.

## 1. Why this unit comes first

User has reported that explaining/correcting can begin automatically and that during an initial calm STOP rehearsal the most uncertain part was **P: Proceed mindfully**. A pause is only useful insofar as it creates a real choice. This unit introduces the attention, Wise Mind, interpersonal-option and behavior-analysis prerequisites previously omitted or underdeveloped. **No deliberate, complete real-world application of STOP is established; a later retrospectively reported spontaneous STOP-like interruption is distinguished below.** Current priorities may change at Monday review.

**Current evidence clarification (October 17):** In a reconstructed real-world misunderstanding, the user reported noticing repeated explaining *during* the event, stopping it, and attempting a practical next action without consciously running through S-T-O-P. This is evidence of a **self-reported STOP-like spontaneous interruption**, not deliberate complete STOP use, a trained in-session rehearsal, mastery, or a reliable frequency trend. It does not change the unit's assigned-but-incomplete status or prove that this method should be the default in every conflict. A single relationship incident is an illustration, not the primary context of this broad program.

**Learning outcomes**, each separately evaluated:
1. Describe the role of emotions, reasonable information, and Wise Mind without claiming any emotion must disappear.
2. Demonstrate Observe/Describe (observable fact vs interpretation vs internal urge) on ordinary examples.
3. Explain and deliberately rehearse all four STOP steps, including recognition *mid-sentence*.
4. Select and actually rehearse a **context-appropriate next response** (listening, inquiry, acknowledgment, concise accurate correction, boundary, repair, or appropriate pause) without assuming agreement.
5. Use selected **Fruzzetti Ch. 3** preparation skills (anticipated cue, urge, substitute action; optionally ending a spiraling interaction); not all practice must concern severe conflict.
6. Distinguish **Missing-Links** for a needed effective action *not taken* from **Chain Analysis** for an ineffective action *actually taken*; use a concrete example to try the correct tool.
7. Compare ACT defusion/values-directed choice as a complement, and identify whether deeper ACT training is needed **now or later**, without prematurely expanding every reading.

## 3. Coherent learning path (stages, not fixed days)

### Stage A — The information needed before STOP's P

**Optional teaching videos (DBT; supplements assigned readings):**
- Wise Mind: [Dr. Jennifer May — Three States of Mind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wlBFV61IG3Q)
- Observe/Describe/Participate: [Dr. Jennifer May — Mindfulness What Skills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TlrIi3V50Qs)
- Nonjudgmentally/One-Mindfully/Effectively: [Dr. Jennifer May — Mindfulness How Skills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1vWsgg9JiDo)


**Wise Mind** — Read Linehan Handouts 2–3a and Fruzzetti Ch. 2's Emotion/Reason/Wise discussion. Contrast: emotionally urgent action; only-facts/only-rules action; a response responsive to facts *and* emotion and long-term goals. Wise Mind does not guarantee consensus or tell you what the other person meant. Demonstrate with a mild real-world or fictional workplace misunderstanding.

**Observe / Describe** — Practice separating (a) visible/audible events, (b) thought/interpretation, (c) feeling if known, (d) urge, (e) action already started. A useful rehearsal may be: *heard a correction to my work; thought “they think I am incompetent”; felt tense; wanted to explain; had begun speaking.* These are illustrative, not claimed user responses. Practice one neutral example and one interpersonal example. Describing is not passive surrender.

**Nonjudgmentally / Effectively** — Observe without adding labels; ask whether an action works for the situation. Nonjudgment does not prevent judging actual safety or maintaining a boundary. Fruzzetti's Ch. 2 preparation includes attention to another person's words and cues without presuming their motive.

### Stage B — STOP as an interruption, including after a false start

**Optional teaching video:** [Dr. Jennifer May — Distress Tolerance STOP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Od9GaNk9Clk).


Read the exact Handout 4. Rehearse both **before replying** and **mid-reply**, when the first explanation has already happened. Stopping *the next sentence* still counts as interrupting further automatic behavior. *Observe* includes both the individual's reaction and the other person's actual communication. Do not equate one pause in chat with a changed real-world pattern.

**P is a selection point, not a canned script.** Evaluate: What are my immediate and longer-term goals? Is any practical fact disputed? Do I need more information? What matters to the other person, as expressed (not guessed)? Is there a safety/boundary issue? What next action would be workable? Choose one context-appropriate behavior rather than a universally pleasant-sounding answer.

### Stage C — Fruzzetti's prevention and rehearsal

**Optional related DBT video:** [Dr. Jennifer May — Urge Surfing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RIA2ewXayTc). This demonstrates a related DBT practice; it is **not** presented as an official Fruzzetti Chapter 3 video or a replacement for his text.


After Ch. 2 foundations, read Ch. 3 and identify its practices as separate from STOP:
- Recognize a **predictable trigger/cue** and notice the tendency to do a familiar action.
- Recall the **effect of following the old response** versus a more constructive one (both immediate and later; do not claim a partner must reciprocate).
- **Rehearse an alternative** repeatedly under manageable conditions, including ordinary response options.
- **Notice an urge** without immediately acting; choose among an effective alternative, legitimate break, or boundary if indicated.
- If relevant, prepare *in the user's own words* a brief way to interrupt an escalating exchange; do not force a lengthy script or unnecessary disengagement.

### Stage D — Analyze the missed or unhelpful action correctly

**Video status:** No Dr. Jennifer May video specifically teaching Linehan Missing-Links Analysis was verified in the selected video catalog. Use the primary Linehan handout/worksheet and guided case analysis rather than assigning a loosely related video.


**Missing-Links**: apply after there was a specific intended action that did not occur (e.g., had intended to listen through another person's account but replied prematurely; or did not perform an agreed everyday action). Follow Linehan Handout 8 in order: knowing the required action, willingness, remembering it in time, obstacles to execution. Avoid inventing the cause.

**Chain Analysis**: apply when the concern is a behavior that did occur and could be changed (e.g., extended defensive explanation). Use Linehan Handouts 6–7a for an educational overview; a formal analysis is only necessary when useful and with an actual event. A self-reported or reconstructed chain is not independent proof of someone else's motive.

### Stage E — Compare, integrate, and decide what to practice next

Briefly compare ACT Choice Point/defusion/valued action with STOP/Wise Mind/Fruzzetti. Explain *what each adds* and what would be learned later. Determine whether the observed gap is **attention, interruption, choice, communication response, memory, willingness, or execution**. Choose one or two rehearsal types as the main short-term practice, not every listed technique simultaneously. Continue in later calendar weeks if needed.

## 3a. How to deliver the unit without assessment drift

**This is a reference curriculum, not a requirement to deliver the entire document in one sitting.** The study/rehearsal session and its practical next move should remain explicit. Choose only the next prerequisite or obstacle; a deep source lesson may be substantial when asked for, but length alone is not learning.

- **Start:** Verify the chosen lesson stage, what was *actually* learned/rehearsed previously, and the one capability to work on today. Use a short reminder or recall of one previously taught step where useful, not a full repeat of the guide.
- **Teach:** Explain the named source skill, its purpose, steps, common obstacles/limits, one ordinary illustration and an optional reading. Ask whether the user has a question **before** treating explanation as a rehearsal. Never substitute a four-model combined script for a named method.
- **Rehearse:** On readiness, choose **one** brief neutral/work/everyday scenario and obtain an actual spoken, typed, or enacted response. Offer specific feedback, then one repeat if useful. Avoid turning a fictional reaction or a proposed response into verified live change.
- **Apply or defer:** Select one doable real-world noticing/action opportunity where wanted, with an observable criterion. A user who wants reflection or further instruction need not accept a plan. Do not assign all scenarios, worksheets, videos and methods simultaneously.
- **Exit:** Summarize the difference between *explained*, *actually rehearsed*, *real-life reported*, and *unknown*. Name the next focused stage (or defer). Log only on an appropriate `Close Session` request. Do not reopen a long case analysis after practice unless new consequential evidence demands it.

**When a real incident arises in a teaching session:** check relevant history, ask only a few decision-relevant questions (roughly 2–4 as a flexible guide), identify an evidence-labeled tentative mechanism, and stop when further remembered details won't change the response to teach or rehearse. If the user is working in a **Program Development** conversation, use the event only to audit design and move further incident work into a focused chat. Detailed event analysis, RO Journal, homework, and weekly review maintain their own purposes. Refer to `Personal Growth Operating Protocol.md` v1.6 for controlling boundaries and exceptions.

**Incident Review method gate:** When a real event is important enough for separate assessment, use the optional Incident / Issue Review prompt and current Operating Protocol. Do **not** call an ordinary short discussion an official behavior chain. For an ineffective action actually taken, a *full* Linehan Chain Analysis may be appropriate; for a planned effective action not performed, consider the *actual* Linehan Missing-Links Analysis. If only an interpersonal interpretation, positive exception, or next-step choice is at stake, a brief contextual review may suffice. Formal source analysis can exceed the ordinary 2–4-question guideline when the steps are consequential. Keep a training chat focused on actually learning the skill.

## 4. Rehearsal library (diverse contexts; optional choices)

A rehearsal consists of a specific **cue** followed by an actual user response (said, typed, or physically practiced), feedback, and possibly another attempt. Merely proposing how one *might* act is discussion, not rehearsal. The app's buttons copy prompts; they do not perform, grade, or save rehearsal.

| R | Scenario, skill source and purpose | Successful *practice evidence* to record (not an assumed outcome) |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 — Noticing and describing** | Neutral work feedback or minor misunderstanding. Linehan Observe/Describe + Fruzzetti Ch. 2. | What was observable, what interpretation appeared, what reaction was noticed, without supplying motives or forced emotional labels. |
| **R2 — Choosing after STOP** | A coworker challenges an estimate or a friend disagrees. STOP + Wise Mind; decide whether to ask, listen, clarify, correct briefly, or postpone. | Actual pause, awareness, choice and a plausible next statement/action. Disagreement remains possible. |
| **R3 — Already started explaining** | Someone says an ordinary comment that feels inaccurate; the user has already begun to justify. STOP mid-response plus a selected continuation. | Where they noticed and what they genuinely said next; not automatic cancellation of the original point. |
| **R4 — Fruzzetti's cue → alternative** | Choose an everyday familiar irritation (without presuming spouse, intensity or cause). Identify the cue and rehearsal alternative; repeat. | Cue identified, old option, alternative rehearsed, and whether attention shifted. No requirement to feel calm. |
| **R5 — Missed intended behavior analysis** | A modest intended action did not occur: e.g. didn't make a follow-up call or missed the intended use of STOP. Linehan Missing-Links. | The user works through knowing/willingness/remembering/execution and selects a barrier-matched next cue or adjustment. This is **analysis practice**, not a scripted response rehearsal. |
| **R6 — Appropriate disagreement / boundary** | Another person persists in a disputed interpretation. Wise Mind + Fruzzetti/ACT contextual selection, respectful self-respect. | Actual response showing whether to continue, ask for clarification, state own view, or establish a legitimate boundary. |
| **R7 — Optional strong activation** | Use *only if requested or supported by current observations*. STOP and Fruzzetti non-escalation. | One genuine non-retaliatory option or boundary; never assume enduring abuse or an obligation to stay engaged. |

**Practice dose:** Initially brief, repeatable attempts (e.g., 2–5 minutes, a few times in the week *after instruction*), adapted to actual feasibility. Don't force every rehearsal once. Include at least one non-conflict or positive/ordinary scenario and don't equate calm simulation with live skill. The Monday review counts activities only from user reports or logs, and distinguishes an opportunity not recognized from missing data.

## 4a. Within-unit spiral practice and future returns

**Sequence, not a rigid calendar:** Introduce the essential components in a manageable order. Once a component has actually been studied, start a subsequent sitting with a short unaided recall/demonstration before giving feedback. Example progression (only as instruction reaches each stage):

1. **After Observe/Describe and Wise Mind:** Briefly recall what counts as observable versus an interpretation, then practice on a *different ordinary example*.
2. **When teaching STOP:** Retrieve the Wise Mind/description foundation; practice a pause, then an actual *P* response, rather than stopping at the first three letters. If the foundation is not yet understood, refresh it instead of labeling retrieval a failure.
3. **When teaching Fruzzetti Chapter 3:** Recall an earlier STOP/Wise Mind response, then rehearse cue → urge → less-automatic action in a second context. Do not teach a fictional mixed-modality protocol as official Fruzzetti or DBT.
4. **When applying Missing-Links:** Recall the difference from Chain Analysis; analyze a *not-done* example, then revisit one previously taught response skill if relevant. Analysis practice and response rehearsal remain distinct.
5. **At Monday review and after this unit:** Select at most one or two specific earlier skills for return in the next relevant learning unit (or another sitting in this same one); use spaced practice and progressively varied ordinary scenarios. A future unit can practice STOP or Wise Mind again without reopening or completing this unit anew.

**Small optional integrated R8 — Retrieval then rehearsal:** Ask for an unaided one-sentence description or one performed step of a previously *taught* method (e.g., Wise Mind or STOP). Give source-based corrective feedback. Then practice one short ordinary scenario that includes this skill plus the current *already-taught* component. When there is only one studied skill, simply vary its context. Record actual responses, not skill acquisition assumptions. This is a **custom curriculum exercise**, not a named treatment protocol.

**Practical dose:** A current-skill focused rehearsal plus one short earlier-skill review when useful; no compulsory daily rotation and no requirement to rehearse all seven/eight scenario options. Missing practice, unknown opportunity and no recognized opportunity are different observations. Retrieval accuracy in chat is not evidence of real-world generalization.

## 6. Stopping point, continuity, and noncompletion

The learner can ask questions at any stage before rehearsal; move gradually and ask one focused question during guided practice. Do not use repeated incident questions as a substitute for completing an educational or rehearsal stage. A phase shift from teaching to practice should be apparent to the user, not inferred as consent. A useful stopping point is reached when the current skill or decision has been adequately explored and a relevant next stage is identified, rather than when every detail or listed exercise has been covered. At a natural stopping point use `Close Session` to record only material actually studied and actual responses rehearsed; closing the session does *not* close or delete a deep-dive chat. A future learning chat can resume the same unit after retrieving the continuity log. Monday reviews can decide **continue Learning Unit 1** rather than creating Unit 2. This unit isn't 'done' because an app checkbox was checked, a lesson was generated, or the user finished reading one handout.
