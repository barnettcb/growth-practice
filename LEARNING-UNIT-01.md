## 3. Coherent learning path (stages, not fixed days)

### Stage A — The information needed before STOP's P

**Optional teaching videos (DBT; supplements assigned readings):**
- Wise Mind: [Dr. Jennifer May — Three States of Mind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wlBFV61IG3Q)
- Observe/Describe/Participate: [Dr. Jennifer May — Mindfulness What Skills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TlrIi3V50Qs)
- Nonjudgmentally/One-Mindfully/Effectively: [Dr. Jennifer May — Mindfulness How Skills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1vWsgg9JiDo)


**Wise Mind** — Use Linehan Handouts 2–3a. Contrast acting from an urgent emotion alone, relying on reason alone, and drawing on emotion, facts and values to respond effectively. Do not reduce Reasonable Mind to rigid rules or Wise Mind to a fixed 50/50 blend. A mild ordinary hypothetical suffices; do not infer the learner must experience a particular emotion.

**Observe / Describe** — Practice separating (a) visible/audible events, (b) thought/interpretation, (c) feeling if known, (d) urge, (e) action already started. A useful rehearsal may be: *heard a correction to my work; thought “they think I am incompetent”; felt tense; wanted to explain; had begun speaking.* These are illustrative, not claimed user responses. Practice one neutral example and one interpersonal example. Describing is not passive surrender.

**Nonjudgmentally / One-Mindfully / Effectively** — Notice what is present without unnecessary evaluations; return attention to the current activity when distracted; choose action suited to current goals and context. Do not infer another person's intentions, treat accurate explanation as automatically defensive, assume shorter communication is better, or equate effectiveness with agreeableness. Nonjudgment does not prevent recognizing harm or maintaining a boundary.

**Participate — remaining brief lesson:** Teach Linehan's third *What* skill directly: engage fully in the activity at hand rather than only observing/describing it. Distinguish Participate from One-Mindfully (the *How* of attending to one activity at a time). Give one ordinary-life example and invite a brief actual response or practice; do not claim it was already completed.

### Stage B — STOP as an interruption, including after a false start

**Optional teaching video:** [Dr. Jennifer May — Distress Tolerance STOP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Od9GaNk9Clk).


Read the exact Handout 4. Rehearse both **before replying** and **mid-reply**, when the first explanation has already happened. Stopping *the next sentence* still counts as interrupting further automatic behavior. *Observe* includes both the individual's reaction and the other person's actual communication. Do not equate one pause in chat with a changed real-world pattern.

**P is a selection point, not a canned script.** Evaluate: What are my immediate and longer-term goals? Is any practical fact disputed? Do I need more information? What matters to the other person, as expressed (not guessed)? Is there a safety/boundary issue? What next action would be workable? Choose one context-appropriate behavior rather than a universally pleasant-sounding answer.

### Stage C — Integrated retrieval and response rehearsal (custom teaching exercise)

**No new reading or stand-alone skill requirement.** Use already taught Linehan skills to make interruption and next-response selection usable in ordinary life. A repeated cue-to-choice drill is a **custom instructional exercise**, not an official blended treatment procedure or a named Linehan method.

- Start with a common but not presumed personal situation (neutral, positive or mildly inconvenient); do **not** assume an urge or emotion that the user does not report.
- Notice what actually happens or is said; distinguish facts, interpretations, possible sensations, thoughts and urges.
- When helpful, rehearse STOP **before** responding or **after** a response has already begun. The *P* action depends on the goal: e.g., listen, ask, clarify, accurately explain, acknowledge, set a boundary, repair, or defer.
- Ask for an **actual typed, spoken or enacted** response in the exercise. Review its fit to the situation rather than requiring shorter, warmer or less explanatory wording by default.
- Revisit earlier material briefly at a later sitting as spiral practice. A calm response is rehearsal evidence, **not** a verified real-world interruption.
- The October 21 `PG-20261021-01` session already includes a relevant guided STOP/Missing-Links rehearsal. **Do not repeat an equivalent beginner rehearsal as a completion gate.** Further practice should test a genuinely different question or occur through natural real-world opportunities.

Optional future expansion: If difficulty recalling or performing a chosen alternative persists, consider separately teaching Linehan's Cope Ahead after checking its source. It is **not required** in LU1 and is not a label for completed work.

### Stage D — Analyze the missed or unhelpful action correctly

**Video status:** No Dr. Jennifer May video specifically teaching Linehan Missing-Links Analysis was verified in the selected video catalog. Use the primary Linehan handout/worksheet and guided case analysis rather than assigning a loosely related video.


**Missing-Links**: apply after there was a specific intended action that did not occur (e.g., had intended to listen through another person's account but replied prematurely; or did not perform an agreed everyday action). Follow Linehan Handout 8 in order: knowing the required action, willingness, remembering it in time, obstacles to execution. Avoid inventing the cause.

**Chain Analysis**: apply when the concern is a behavior that did occur and could be changed (e.g., extended defensive explanation). Use Linehan Handouts 6–7a for an educational overview; a formal analysis is only necessary when useful and with an actual event. A self-reported or reconstructed chain is not independent proof of someone else's motive.

## 3a. How to deliver the unit without assessment drift

**This is a reference curriculum, not a requirement to deliver the entire document in one sitting.** The study/rehearsal session and its practical next move should remain explicit. Choose only the next prerequisite or obstacle; a deep source lesson may be substantial when asked for, but length alone is not learning.

- **Start:** Verify the chosen lesson stage, what was *actually* learned/rehearsed previously, and the one capability to work on today. Use a short reminder or recall of one previously taught step where useful, not a full repeat of the guide.
- **Teach:** Explain the named source skill, its purpose, steps, common obstacles/limits, one ordinary illustration and an optional reading. Ask whether the user has a question **before** treating explanation as a rehearsal. Never substitute a four-model combined script for a named method.
- **Rehearse:** On readiness, choose **one** brief ordinary scenario not tailored to the learner's job or personal relationships unless requested, and obtain an actual spoken, typed, or enacted response. Offer specific feedback, then one repeat if useful. Avoid turning a fictional reaction or a proposed response into verified live change.
- **Apply or defer:** Select one doable real-world noticing/action opportunity where wanted, with an observable criterion. A user who wants reflection or further instruction need not accept a plan. Do not assign all scenarios, worksheets, videos and methods simultaneously.
- **Exit:** Summarize the difference between *explained*, *actually rehearsed*, *real-life reported*, and *unknown*. Name the next focused stage (or defer). Log only on an appropriate `Close Session` request. Do not reopen a long case analysis after practice unless new consequential evidence demands it.

**When a real incident arises in a teaching session:** check relevant history, ask only a few decision-relevant questions (roughly 2–4 as a flexible guide), identify an evidence-labeled tentative mechanism, and stop when further remembered details won't change the response to teach or rehearse. If the user is working in a **Program Development** conversation, use the event only to audit design and move further incident work into a focused chat. Detailed event analysis, RO Journal, homework, and weekly review maintain their own purposes. Refer to the **current** `Personal Growth Operating Protocol.md` for controlling boundaries and exceptions.

**Incident Review method gate:** When a real event is important enough for separate assessment, use the optional Incident / Issue Review prompt and current Operating Protocol. Do **not** call an ordinary short discussion an official behavior chain. For an ineffective action actually taken, a *full* Linehan Chain Analysis may be appropriate; for a planned effective action not performed, consider the *actual* Linehan Missing-Links Analysis. If only an interpersonal interpretation, positive exception, or next-step choice is at stake, a brief contextual review may suffice. Formal source analysis can exceed the ordinary 2–4-question guideline when the steps are consequential. Keep a training chat focused on actually learning the skill.

## 4. Rehearsal library (diverse contexts; optional choices)

A rehearsal consists of a specific **cue** and the user's actual response (said, typed, physically practiced, or otherwise reported), feedback, and optional retry. A proposed scenario is not evidence the learner performed the response. Examples are **generic everyday situations**; do not automatically insert the user's work, partner, or historical experiences.

| R | Scenario, source and purpose | Evidence to record (without assuming success) |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 — Notice and describe** | Friend changes plans unexpectedly. Linehan Observe/Describe and Nonjudgmentally. | Distinguish observable event, possible interpretations and actual internal reactions, without inventing motives. |
| **R2 — Choosing after STOP** | Someone asks for a favor when time is limited. Linehan STOP, Wise Mind, Effectively. | Where/when a pause is attempted and one actual next response consistent with real constraints and priorities. |
| **R3 — Interruption mid-response** | During ordinary conversation, an explanation begins before full listening. Linehan STOP and One-Mindfully. | Recognition timing, interruption if useful, and a chosen continuation; no rule that explaining itself is wrong. |
| **R4 — Optional cue-to-choice variation** | A minor unexpected request or reminder. **Custom spiral rehearsal using already-taught Linehan skills.** | The cue, any noticed urge, actual alternative response, and any uncertainty; do not count the October 21 attempt twice. |
| **R5 — Missing effective action** | An intended ordinary activity did not happen. Linehan Missing-Links. | Sequential checks of knowledge, willingness, timely remembering and execution only as applicable; this is analysis practice, not an enacted response. |
| **R6 — Appropriate disagreement or boundary** | Friend holds a different view or presses an unwanted request. Linehan Wise Mind / Effectively. | A chosen response suited to the user's goals; neither forced agreement nor forced confrontation. |
| **R7 — Optional higher activation** | Only if requested or supported by evidence. Linehan STOP. | Actual response and limits; never presume a particular emotional intensity or that remaining engaged is required. |

**Practice dose:** Brief, repeatable attempts when actually useful (e.g., a few minutes), not a mandatory checklist. At least one ordinary non-conflict or positive example is desirable over time. Reuse prior completed attempts rather than duplicating them for lesson credit. Monday review distinguishes missing data, no recognized opportunity, and known absence of action.

## 4a. Within-unit spiral practice and future returns

**Sequence, not a rigid calendar:** Introduce the essential components in a manageable order. Once a component has actually been studied, start a subsequent sitting with a short unaided recall/demonstration before giving feedback. Example progression (only as instruction reaches each stage):

1. **After Observe/Describe and Wise Mind:** Briefly recall what counts as observable versus an interpretation, then practice on a *different ordinary example*.
2. **When teaching STOP:** Retrieve the Wise Mind/description foundation; practice a pause, then an actual *P* response, rather than stopping at the first three letters. If the foundation is not yet understood, refresh it instead of labeling retrieval a failure.
3. **When reviewing integrated response choice:** Recall an earlier STOP/Wise Mind response, then use a new ordinary context only if it tests a new decision. Label the cue-to-choice structure a custom teaching exercise, not a newly named treatment protocol.
4. **When applying Missing-Links:** Recall the difference from Chain Analysis; analyze a *not-done* example, then revisit one previously taught response skill if relevant. Analysis practice and response rehearsal remain distinct.
5. **At Monday review and after this unit:** Select at most one or two specific earlier skills for return in the next relevant learning unit (or another sitting in this same one); use spaced practice and progressively varied ordinary scenarios. A future unit can practice STOP or Wise Mind again without reopening or completing this unit anew.

**Small optional integrated R8 — Retrieval then rehearsal:** Ask for an unaided one-sentence description or one performed step of a previously *taught* method (e.g., Wise Mind or STOP). Give source-based corrective feedback. Then practice one short ordinary scenario that includes this skill plus the current *already-taught* component. When there is only one studied skill, simply vary its context. Record actual responses, not skill acquisition assumptions. This is a **custom curriculum exercise**, not a named treatment protocol.

**Practical dose:** A current-skill focused rehearsal plus one short earlier-skill review when useful; no compulsory daily rotation and no requirement to rehearse all seven/eight scenario options. Missing practice, unknown opportunity and no recognized opportunity are different observations. Retrieval accuracy in chat is not evidence of real-world generalization.

## 6. Stopping point, continuity, and noncompletion

The learner can ask questions at any stage before rehearsal; move gradually and ask one focused question during guided practice. Do not use repeated incident questions as a substitute for completing an educational or rehearsal stage. A phase shift from teaching to practice should be apparent to the user, not inferred as consent. A useful stopping point is reached when the current skill or decision has been adequately explored and a relevant next stage is identified, rather than when every detail or listed exercise has been covered. At a natural stopping point use `Close Session` to record only material actually studied and actual responses rehearsed; closing the session does *not* close or delete a deep-dive chat. **LU1 foundational instruction and initial guided rehearsal were marked complete by the user on October 27, after Participate and integration.** The active substantive Skills Training session is **not automatically closed** by this curriculum decision; the separate `Close Session` trigger governs its continuity record. Ongoing real-world application, reliability, and generalization remain unverified and continue through later units and Monday reviews.
